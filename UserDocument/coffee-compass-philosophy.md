# Circular Taxonomy

Communication through radial symmetry and chromatic gradients that map human sensory experience onto geometric precision. The compass rose becomes a knowledge system — each direction a pathway, each color a signal, each word a waypoint in navigational space.

Form radiates from a singular center of perfection outward into zones of deviation. The geometry is deliberate: concentric rings suggest proximity to ideal, while angular divisions create categorical boundaries that feel natural rather than imposed. Every arc is placed with the precision of a cartographer charting invisible territories, the product of countless hours of spatial calibration.

Color operates as a functional language — warm tones signal excess and intensity, cool tones signal deficiency and absence. The palette is restrained yet purposeful: each hue earns its place through informational necessity. Transitions between zones are smooth, suggesting that flavor space is continuous, not discrete. This chromatic system is meticulously crafted to feel intuitive at first glance while rewarding sustained study.

Typography exists as navigational markers — small, precise, clinical. Chinese characters bring density and visual weight that Latin script cannot achieve, each glyph a compact information vessel. Text is positioned with painstaking attention to radial alignment, following the compass's angular logic. Labels whisper rather than shout, serving the spatial architecture rather than competing with it.

The overall composition achieves the paradox of making the subjective feel scientific — taste mapped with the rigor of celestial navigation. This is master-level execution of information design: where data visualization meets sensory poetry, where a brewing adjustment guide becomes an artifact worthy of framing. Every element placed as if by someone at the absolute top of their field, laboring over each degree of rotation, each pixel of spacing.
